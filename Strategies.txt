MARKET MAKING / TRADING STRATEGY REFERENCE
=========================================

Purpose
-------
This file is a quick reference for different trading and market making strategies,
what kind of product behavior they fit, and where they appeared in earlier winning-style bots.

--------------------------------------------------
1. SIMPLE MARKET MAKING STRATEGIES
--------------------------------------------------

1.1 Static Fair-Value Market Making
-----------------------------------
Idea:
- Assume the product has a stable fair value.
- Buy below fair value.
- Sell above fair value.
- Quote around the fair value and collect spread.

Used when:
- Product is strongly anchored.
- Price repeatedly returns to one stable level.
- Mid does not trend much.

Good for:
- Static products like EMERALDS / RAINFOREST_RESIN type behavior.

Typical logic:
- Fair = fixed anchor or very slow moving average.
- Take if ask << fair or bid >> fair.
- Otherwise quote bid/ask around fair.

Main risk:
- If the product suddenly trends, static fair becomes wrong.

Seen in earlier winner code:
- StaticTrader for RAINFOREST_RESIN.


1.2 Join / Improve Market Making
--------------------------------
Idea:
- Do not always quote from scratch.
- Look at current best bid/ask and either:
  - join them
  - improve by 1 tick
  - stay wider if needed

Used when:
- Book is stable.
- Spread is meaningful.
- Queue position matters.

Good for:
- General market making.
- Products with reliable book structure.

Typical logic:
- Bid = best_bid + 1 if still safe.
- Ask = best_ask - 1 if still safe.

Main risk:
- Too close to the touch can lead to passive pickoffs.


1.3 Inventory-Skewed Market Making
----------------------------------
Idea:
- Shift quotes depending on inventory.
- If too long, quote more aggressively on sell side.
- If too short, quote more aggressively on buy side.

Used when:
- Product fills often.
- Inventory risk matters.
- You do not want to sit with large unwanted exposure.

Good for:
- Almost all market making products.

Typical logic:
- Fair_adj = fair - inventory * skew
or
- Move quote center away from the side that would worsen inventory.

Main risk:
- Too much skew reduces participation.
- Too little skew lets inventory run away.


1.4 Spread-Capture Only MM
--------------------------
Idea:
- Ignore forecasting almost completely.
- Just capture spread around a stable center.

Used when:
- Price prediction is weak.
- Product is mostly stationary.
- Spread capture is the main edge.

Good for:
- Stable, non-trending products.

Main risk:
- Gets run over in directional moves.


--------------------------------------------------
2. SIMPLE DIRECTIONAL / SIGNAL STRATEGIES
--------------------------------------------------

2.1 Mean Reversion
------------------
Idea:
- If price moves too far from recent average, expect it to come back.

Used when:
- Product stretches away from short-term fair and often snaps back.
- Local overreaction is common.

Good for:
- Range-bound products.
- Products with local deviations but weak long trends.

Typical logic:
- If price > MA + threshold -> sell.
- If price < MA - threshold -> buy.

Main risk:
- Dangerous in real strong trends.

Seen in winner code:
- Underlying mean reversion.
- Options mean reversion.


2.2 Trend Following / Momentum
------------------------------
Idea:
- If market is moving with strong confirmation, trade in that direction.

Used when:
- Product trends intraday.
- Microprice / imbalance / flow confirm continuation.

Good for:
- Dynamic products like TOMATOES / KELP style behavior.

Typical logic:
- If predicted next move is up -> buy / quote more bullish.
- If predicted next move is down -> sell / quote more bearish.

Main risk:
- Chasing too late.
- Buying tops / selling bottoms.


2.3 Informed Trader Following
-----------------------------
Idea:
- Detect a known informed participant.
- Follow or adapt to their recent trading direction.

Used when:
- The simulator has recurring informed agents.
- Their actions predict near-future moves.

Good for:
- Products with identifiable informed flow.

Typical logic:
- If informed trader recently bought -> bias long.
- If informed trader recently sold -> bias short.

Main risk:
- Signal decays fast.
- If late, you become exit liquidity.

Seen in winner code:
- DynamicTrader / InkTrader using Olivia.


--------------------------------------------------
3. ADVANCED MARKET MAKING FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------

3.1 Avellaneda-Stoikov (AS)
---------------------------
Idea:
- Inventory-aware market making framework.
- Computes reservation price and optimal spread from:
  - fair price
  - inventory
  - volatility
  - time remaining
  - risk aversion

Used when:
- You want a clean inventory / quote-control model.
- You already have a good fair value estimate.

Good for:
- Quote control.
- Risk control layer on top of another alpha model.

Best use:
- As a secondary quote-control overlay, not always as the main alpha engine.

Main risk:
- Can become too conservative.
- Often underperforms if passive fill quality and alpha matter more than inventory theory.

Important note:
- Best for controlling quotes.
- Not enough alone if the product edge is mostly from flow/trend signals.


3.2 GLFT (Guéant-Lehalle-Fernandez-Tapia)
-----------------------------------------
Idea:
- Similar family as AS but often more practical for quoting / inventory approximations.

Used when:
- You like AS-style inventory-aware MM.
- You want a more usable control-layer variant.

Good for:
- Quote control and inventory smoothing.

Main risk:
- Still mainly a control model, not a full predictive trading model.


3.3 Queue-Aware Market Making
-----------------------------
Idea:
- The value of a passive quote depends on queue position and fill probability.
- Being first or early in queue matters.

Used when:
- Passive fills matter a lot.
- You get picked off or fill too slowly.
- Quote placement quality matters more than raw fair value.

Good for:
- Products where passive quality is critical.

Typical logic:
- Estimate fill probability by:
  - distance from touch
  - queue size
  - side
  - spread
  - toxicity
- Post only if expected passive EV is positive.

Main risk:
- Needs good fill attribution and statistics.


3.4 Alpha-Aware Market Making
-----------------------------
Idea:
- Mix directional alpha with market making.
- Quote center / skew depends on short-term signal.

Used when:
- Product has directional microstructure signal.
- You still want to collect spread while leaning with the market.

Good for:
- Products like TOMATOES.
- Dynamic but still market-makable products.

Typical logic:
- Fair = mid + alpha
- Reservation / quotes shift with signal
- Taker and maker logic separated

Main risk:
- Double-counting alpha.
- Too aggressive quote centering can worsen passive selection.


--------------------------------------------------
4. RELATIVE VALUE / ARBITRAGE STRATEGIES
--------------------------------------------------

4.1 ETF Basket Arbitrage
------------------------
Idea:
- Compare ETF price with weighted sum of constituents.
- If ETF too expensive -> short ETF, buy constituents.
- If ETF too cheap -> buy ETF, short constituents.

Used when:
- A basket product is derived from components.
- Weight relationship is stable.

Good for:
- PICNIC_BASKET style products.

Typical logic:
- Basket spread = ETF price - weighted constituent price
- Trade when spread exceeds threshold

Main risk:
- Hedge ratio wrong.
- Constituent liquidity mismatch.

Seen in winner code:
- EtfTrader.


4.2 Cross-Product Hedged Relative Value
---------------------------------------
Idea:
- Trade one asset and hedge with another related asset.

Used when:
- Two products are linked but not identical.
- One lags or deviates from the other.

Good for:
- Basket/constituent relationships.
- Option/underlying relationships.

Main risk:
- Hedge imperfect.
- Correlation breaks.


4.3 Conversion Arbitrage
------------------------
Idea:
- Compare local price versus external / converted price after fees.
- Trade only when the conversion economics are positive.

Used when:
- Simulator includes conversion observations.
- Transport / tariffs / fees exist.

Good for:
- Import/export style products.

Typical logic:
- Compute local buy/sell versus external bid/ask after costs.
- Trade when arbitrage > threshold.

Main risk:
- Fees or conversions remove apparent edge.
- Overtrading small fake arbitrage.

Seen in winner code:
- CommodityTrader for MAGNIFICENT_MACARONS.


--------------------------------------------------
5. OPTIONS / DERIVATIVES STRATEGIES
--------------------------------------------------

5.1 Black-Scholes Theoretical Value Trading
-------------------------------------------
Idea:
- Use option pricing model to compute theoretical value.
- Compare market option price with theory.

Used when:
- Options exist and underlying price is available.
- Vol estimate is usable.

Good for:
- Option chains around one underlying.

Typical logic:
- If option market price > theoretical -> sell option.
- If option market price < theoretical -> buy option.

Main risk:
- Wrong volatility estimate makes theory useless.


5.2 Implied Volatility (IV) Scalping
------------------------------------
Idea:
- Compare current option mispricing against a rolling mean of theoretical deviation.
- Trade when implied-vol pricing is stretched.

Used when:
- Option prices oscillate around theoretical value.
- Smile / vol structure is reasonably stable.

Good for:
- Liquid options.
- Repeated IV over/underpricing.

Seen in winner code:
- OptionTrader IV scalping logic.


5.3 Option Mean Reversion
-------------------------
Idea:
- Option prices / deviations revert to a recent average.

Used when:
- Option mispricing is temporary.
- Market repeatedly overreacts.

Main risk:
- Underlying move can make option reversion idea wrong.


5.4 Delta / Vega Hedged Option Trading
--------------------------------------
Idea:
- Trade option mispricing and hedge the underlying exposure.

Used when:
- You want purer volatility / mispricing exposure.
- Underlying moves can dominate option PnL.

Good for:
- More advanced option trading setups.

Main risk:
- Hedge lag and transaction cost.


--------------------------------------------------
6. PRODUCT BEHAVIOR -> BEST STRATEGY MATCH
--------------------------------------------------

6.1 Strongly Anchored Static Product
------------------------------------
Behavior:
- Price stays near one stable value.
- Very little drift.
- Spread capture matters more than prediction.

Best strategies:
- Static fair-value MM
- Join / improve MM
- Inventory-skewed MM
- Spread-capture only MM

Example:
- EMERALDS / RAINFOREST_RESIN style.


6.2 Dynamic but Locally Mean-Reverting Product
----------------------------------------------
Behavior:
- Short-term deviations occur often.
- Local snapback is common.
- Can still move intraday.

Best strategies:
- Mean reversion
- Alpha-aware MM
- Inventory-skewed MM
- Passive EV filter

Example:
- TOMATOES if it stretches and then reverts locally.


6.3 Trendy / Flow-Driven Product
--------------------------------
Behavior:
- Strong short-term moves.
- Book imbalance / microprice / OFI matter.
- Trend participation is important.

Best strategies:
- Trend following
- Alpha-aware MM
- Aggressive taking in strong regimes
- Light inventory overlay
- Possibly informed-trader following

Example:
- KELP / TOMATOES trend states.


6.4 Product with Known Informed Flow
------------------------------------
Behavior:
- Certain traders consistently move before price.
- Their prints matter.

Best strategies:
- Informed-trader following
- One-sided quoting in informed direction
- Reduce quoting against informed flow

Example:
- Olivia-based winner logic.


6.5 Basket / Composite Product
------------------------------
Behavior:
- One product should equal weighted sum of others.

Best strategies:
- ETF basket arbitrage
- Relative value spread trading
- Hedge with constituents

Example:
- PICNIC_BASKET1 / PICNIC_BASKET2.


6.6 Option Chain Around Underlying
----------------------------------
Behavior:
- Several strikes linked to one underlying.
- Mispricing can come from IV or smile distortion.

Best strategies:
- BS theoretical value trading
- IV scalping
- Mean reversion in theo deviation
- Delta/vega hedging

Example:
- VOLCANIC_ROCK_VOUCHER products.


6.7 Conversion / External Market Product
----------------------------------------
Behavior:
- Local and external prices linked by fees and tariffs.

Best strategies:
- Conversion arbitrage
- Threshold-based arb with fee adjustment
- Small-size repeated execution

Example:
- MAGNIFICENT_MACARONS.


6.8 Toxic / Highly Adverse Product
----------------------------------
Behavior:
- Passive fills often bad.
- Large jumps or wide spreads.
- You get picked off if you quote too close.

Best strategies:
- Queue-aware MM
- Passive EV veto
- Wider quote placement
- More aggressive filtering
- Less blind spread capture

Main rule:
- Do not force passive MM just because spread looks big.


--------------------------------------------------
7. PRACTICAL STRATEGY BUILDING ORDER
--------------------------------------------------

If starting from scratch, the usual order is:

1. Static fair-value MM
2. Add inventory skew
3. Add join/improve logic
4. Add local mean reversion or trend signal
5. Add passive EV / fill-quality control
6. Add relative value / basket arb if product allows
7. Add options / conversion / informed-flow logic if special structure exists

For most products:
- First solve fair value
- Then solve inventory
- Then solve passive fill quality
- Then solve special product structure


--------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT TO USE FOR OUR OWN PRODUCTS
--------------------------------------------------

EMERALDS
--------
Best fit:
- Static fair-value MM
- Inventory skew
- Join/improve
- Rare aggressive takes when clearly mispriced

Do not overcomplicate.


TOMATOES
--------
Best fit:
- Alpha-aware MM
- Trend + local reversion hybrid
- Passive quality filter
- Queue-aware / fill-quality-aware logic
- Strong taker logic in good trend states

Less useful as main engine:
- Pure Avellaneda-Stoikov alone


--------------------------------------------------
9. KEY LESSON
--------------------------------------------------

No strategy is "best" by name alone.

The right question is always:
"What kind of behavior does this product have?"

Then match the product to the strategy:

- anchored -> static MM
- flow-driven -> alpha-aware MM
- trending -> momentum / taker support
- reverting -> mean reversion
- basket-linked -> relative value / ETF arb
- option-linked -> theoretical value / IV trading
- conversion-linked -> conversion arb
- toxic passive fills -> queue-aware MM and passive EV filters
